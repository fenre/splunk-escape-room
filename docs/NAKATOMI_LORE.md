# Nakatomi Plaza — World & Character Lore

This document is the authoritative in-world lore bible for the Nakatomi
Heist escape room. It fills out the backdrop that players never
strictly need to solve the vault, but that curious analysts — the
kind who actually read the intranet announcements and chase the
diary/schematic/HR-file easter eggs — will find seeded throughout the
generated dataset.

Nothing in this doc changes a single seal, code, or timer. The entire
document is a reference for:

- **Writers** adding new side mysteries, announcements, chat threads,
  or dialogue without contradicting prior-incident continuity.
- **Facilitators** running live sessions who want a 30-second "tell me
  about this building" answer when a team asks.
- **Players** re-reading after a session, the way you re-watch a
  heist film and spot the set dressing.

Events in the generated dataset reference every milestone and every
character in this file. If a new contributor adds an incident or
character, it must land both here and (where appropriate) as a
discoverable event in the dataset.

## 1. Nakatomi Plaza — A Brief History

### 1.1 The Tower

Nakatomi Plaza is a 35-storey steel-and-glass tower at 2121 Avenue
of the Stars, Century City, Los Angeles. Sub-basement B2 holds
employee parking; sub-basement B1 is a secure level that contains
**the vault**, the mechanical plant for the building water supply,
and a rarely used mail-processing room. The roof carries a helipad,
the HVAC chillers, a satellite uplink, and two rows of maintenance
walkways.

- Architect: Yoshio Nakajima & Associates (Tokyo).
- Structural engineer: Caldicott-Owens, Los Angeles.
- Construction: 1974–1977, topped out March 1977.
- Tenant ribbon-cutting: **4 July 1977**.
- Primary tenant and owner: **Nakatomi Trading Corporation** (since
  inception). Floors 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 28, 30, and 35 are all
  occupied by Nakatomi entities. The remaining floors (not listed in
  the in-game floor directory) are leased to ancillary tenants
  whose data does not appear in this scenario.

### 1.2 Building Timeline (milestones)

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1974 | Ground broken on site formerly occupied by the Kumekawa Brothers warehouse. |
| 1977 | Topped out; first tenants move in; **Vault B1** commissioned. |
| 1981 | Elevator core refurbished. Mitsubishi 2600 series cabs installed. |

### 1.3 Nakatomi Trading Corporation — mission

Nakatomi Trading is a Japanese-American conglomerate founded 1953
by Oda Nakatomi (deceased 1984). Its Los Angeles office is the
company's single largest, responsible for **international trade,
currency, and the custody of bearer-bond instruments** on behalf of
clients across the Pacific Rim. **$640 million in bearer bonds** —
the entire Pacific division's Q4 settlement — sits in the B1 vault
on Christmas Eve 1988.

Publicly the company is a trading house. Internally, Nakatomi's
Vault Operations team (floor B1) and Executive Suite (floor 30)
treat the bond custody as the single most sensitive part of the
business. Only clearance LEVEL-5 and above know how much is
currently in the vault; only LEVEL-6 (CEO only) knows the full
seven-seal protocol.

## 2. Prior Incidents

### 2.1 October 1985 — The Car #2 Elevator Fire

**Case reference:** NAK-85-OCT-0194
**Location:** Elevator Car #2, between floors 14 and 15.
**Outcome:** No fatalities. One smoke-inhalation injury. Two weeks
of partial elevator outage. Building-wide fire-suppression retrofit
the following year.

Around 18:40 on **14 October 1985**, Car #2 stalled between floors
14 and 15 with nine people aboard — five Nakatomi staff (all HR,
returning from a late off-site), two paralegals from the floor-5
Legal tenancy, one visiting consultant, and a maintenance tech
named **Eduardo Vasquez** (MAINT-007), who was performing a
routine inspection.

A short in the door-interlock harness had ignited the armoured
cabling inside the elevator shaft. The car filled with smoke over
the next nine minutes. Vasquez kept the occupants calm, broke the
emergency ceiling hatch, ventilated the cab, and talked LAFD
through the rescue when they arrived. The one injury (smoke
inhalation) was the consultant, who had panicked and tried to pry
the doors open.

Lessons learned, still visible in the 1988 dataset:

- All elevator cabs were fitted with independent smoke detectors.
  The building's HVAC Zone 4 inspection cycle (HALF-HVAC events)
  still includes elevator-shaft airflow checks.
- Vasquez was promoted from general maintenance to **Building
  Operations lead** — by 1988 he is the senior member of Facilities,
  badge MAINT-007, and the one person other than the CEO who can
  override the vault-level mechanical plant.
- Fire-suppression retrofits that followed this incident are the
  reason floors 1–20 use halon-style flooding systems while the
  upper floors (added later) use newer non-halon agents. HVAC logs
  from 1988 still show the retrofit zoning.

In the 1988 dataset, an anniversary note lands on the intranet
bulletin board a few days before Christmas Eve; see §5.

### 2.2 December 1987 — The Room 30-B Incident

**Case reference:** NAK-87-DEC-1203
**Location:** Conference Room 30-B, Executive Suite.
**Outcome:** One hospitalisation. Full hostage resolution in 47
minutes. No fatalities. Complete overhaul of floor-30 security
protocols. Promotion of **Joseph Takagi** from COO to CEO.

On **3 December 1987** a disgruntled former trader, Stephen Marsters,
entered Nakatomi Plaza through the main lobby during a Q4 board
meeting. Marsters had been let go on redundancy grounds eight
months prior and had not had his guest-book credential revoked.
He carried a handgun and a hand-held VHF radio, reached the floor
30 executive suite at 14:07, and took seven board members and
two executive assistants hostage in Conference Room 30-B. The
then-CEO Kenji Saito suffered a cardiac event mid-negotiation and
was hospitalised.

Takagi, then COO, refused to evacuate. Over 47 minutes he kept
Marsters talking — on an internal vault-operations radio channel
that happened to be monitored by LAPD liaison **Sergeant Al Powell** —
long enough for a five-person LAPD team to reach floor 30 via the
service stairs. Marsters surrendered without a shot fired.

Lessons learned, still visible in 1988:

- All **ex-employee access was purged within 24 hours of
  termination** as policy. (The Ghost Account side story in 1988
  demonstrates exactly what goes wrong when the policy lapses.)
- Conference Room 30-B was reinforced: secondary lock on the
  boardroom door, panic button below the conference table, a
  disused emergency corridor behind the north wall. A decade later
  that corridor is still on the schematic archive (see easter egg
  `6404` — "The Archive Door").
- **Joseph Takagi** was promoted to CEO three months after the
  incident. His private diary entry from 1987 (never digitised;
  referenced only in HR files) reads: *"I am CEO because a man
  with a gun mistook patience for weakness."*
- **Sergeant Al Powell** remained the LAPD-side liaison for
  Nakatomi security planning. His vault-operations radio channel
  was re-keyed after the 1987 incident and then again in early
  1988. On Christmas Eve 1988, Powell will end up the LAPD liaison
  for a much larger incident — though he does not yet know that.

There are whispers inside Nakatomi (see § 5.6) that the 1987
incident is the reason the 1988 Christmas party was nearly cancelled.
It was not. It should have been.

## 4. Character Bios

Every character listed below appears in the 1988 dataset — most as
badge-swipe or transaction events, some only as text references
in announcements, HR files, chat threads, or schematic annotations.

### 4.1 Executives & Key Staff

**Joseph Takagi** (`JT-0001`, CEO, LEVEL-6) — Promoted from COO in
1988 after the Room 30-B incident. 52 years old, second-generation
Japanese-American, raised in Long Beach. Father worked in the shipyards;
mother in real estate. MBA from Stanford, 1962. Married to Linda, two
children. Widely respected internally as a patient negotiator who
treats every meeting like a chess endgame. Keeps the **only** copy
of the full vault rotation schedule in a locked desk drawer on
floor 30. His calendar on Christmas Eve 1988 lists a Q4 board close
at 20:30 and a return home by 22:00.

**Harry Ellis** (`HE-3301`, International Trade, LEVEL-2) —
"Dealmaker," in his own description. 38, Armani suits, a permanent
tan, and a rolodex he is not shy about. Responsible for the
Nakatomi-Atropos joint venture and the Far-East currency swap desk.
Harry is charismatic, loud, and absolutely certain he can talk his
way out of any situation. The affair with Holly (side story 9012)
is not nearly as secret as he believes.

**Holly Gennero** (`HP-1015`, International Trade, LEVEL-4) — Head
of the L.A. International Trade department. 34, Stanford Law 1978,
joined Nakatomi in 1982 and made department head in 1986 — the
youngest person ever to hold the role. Married (currently
separated) to a New York City police officer named John McClane.
Two children (Lucy and John Jr.) live with her in L.A. Holly
uses her maiden name at work; "Holly Gennero McClane" appears
only on her emergency contact file. Her **diary entry from 24
December 1988** (easter egg `6024`) is the closest thing the
dataset has to an emotional centre.

**Naomi Park** (`NP-4472`, Vault Operations, LEVEL-5) — Vault Ops
lead. 41, ex-US Navy (crypto division 1979–1985), joined Nakatomi
1986 on Takagi's personal recommendation. She is one of only three
people in the building who have the LEVEL-5 clearance to touch the
B1 vault hardware, and the only one reliably in L.A. on Christmas
Eve (the other two are on East Coast assignments). She is on the
Christmas-party guest list but planned to leave by 20:30 to drive
back to Pasadena.

**Argyle** (`AG-0077`, Building Services, LEVEL-1) — Actually a
contracted driver for Alpha Limousine Service; Nakatomi issues a
LEVEL-1 badge for garage-only access. 23 years old, first real
driving job out of high school. Argyle has been loaned to Holly
as a courtesy ride for her sister's visiting partner who is flying
in on Christmas Eve. Spends the evening parked in B2 with the
limo radio, a rum-and-coke, and enough Kurtis Blow on tape to
survive a shift. The carphone in his limo is on channel
`carphone_local` (easter egg `6401`).

### 4.2 Building Security

**James Marsh** (`SE-0012`, Security, LEVEL-3) — Senior duty lead,
floor 1. 47, ex-LAPD (retired with a knee injury 1984). One of two
people on-site who can authorise a main-lobby override, and the
face most employees recognise at the reception desk. On Christmas
Eve 1988 he is on the 14:00–22:00 shift; he hands over to the
night lead at 22:00. His badge swipe at 22:05 in the Seal 1 data
is **not** him — he was subdued in the security office by Karl's
team at 22:02 and the badge was taken.

**Lisa Tanaka** (`SE-0015`, Security, LEVEL-3) — The other senior
lead, opposite rotation. On Christmas Eve she is off-shift, at the
party as a guest of Holly's department. She is not on duty when
the takeover begins. (Her absence is why James Marsh's badge
reappears at the lobby after he's already been subdued.)

**Wesley Aldridge** (`SE-0020`, Security, LEVEL-2) — Morning-shift
guard, 6:00–14:00. 28, started as a mall guard, promoted into
Nakatomi Security last year. Wesley ends the 24 December shift at
14:00 and goes home. His only appearance in the heist dataset is
the handover conversation to the floating afternoon shift.

**Aaron Blackwell** (`SE-0024`, Security, LEVEL-2) — Night-shift
guard, 22:00–06:00. Aaron is the one who was supposed to take
over from James Marsh at 22:00 on Christmas Eve. He arrived at
the building on time; Karl's team met him in the service
corridor. He is the fourth person on the subdued-staff list
reported later that night.

**Cyrus Mahmoudi** (`SE-0026`, Security, LEVEL-3) — Floating
dayshift lead (10:00–18:00). Handles walk-ins, VIP escorts, and
visitor-badge issuance. Cyrus is the one who processes
`GUEST-ROY-ROGERS` (easter egg `6777`) without asking too many
questions, because the sponsor badge looks legitimate and it's
Christmas Eve. He will regret this in his written statement.

### 4.3 Facilities & Janitorial

**Eduardo Vasquez** (`MAINT-007`, Facilities, LEVEL-2) — Building
Operations lead. 58, the longest-serving employee on-site. Hired
as a general maintenance tech in 1977 when the tower opened; hero
of the 1985 elevator fire (§ 2.1); today runs the entire
mechanical plant from a small office on floor 3. Knows where
every pipe, vent, and conduit in the tower runs. His badge
`MAINT-007` is the Seal 1 red-herring for the security office
check — a routine Vasquez swipe an hour before the takeover that
is easily mistaken for the override event.

**Quentin Marquez** (`CLN-001`) and the **cleaning crew** —
janitorial team on the 22:00–02:00 overnight rotation, covering
floors 1–28 on alternating nights. Nakatomi contracts the crew
through a local vendor called **Sparkling Pacific Services**.
None of the cleaning crew were scheduled for floor 30 on
Christmas Eve (the party had overnight catering cover); they
work 1–5 and 10–20 instead. Their badge traffic shows a normal
sweep until about 22:10 when Hans's team cuts lobby access and
the crew is sent home.

### 4.4 Engineering, IT & R&D

**Junko Hanada** (`ENG-2504`, R&D, LEVEL-4) — Senior R&D engineer,
floor 25. 39, Stanford PhD in Electrical Engineering 1982, joined
Nakatomi in 1984. Project lead on the **Y2K remediation pilot**
(side story 9200): an experimental date-rollover test designed
to expose legacy systems that would break when the calendar
ticked to 2000. Junko is meticulous and keeps immaculate logs,
which is precisely why her Dec 22 test event is so easy to find.

**Robert Chen** (`NP-4471`, IT Operations, LEVEL-3) — IT engineer,
floor 25. 34, passed over for a VP slot in October 1988 and on
an HR PIP (side story 9315). Chen's after-hours swipes to the
server room and B1 anteroom are not malicious — they're the
behaviour of a very talented engineer trying to build a case
that he was overlooked. He's also the IT admin who never
revoked the **XG-9999 "ghost account"** belonging to Evan
Rutherford.

**Ivan Krylov** (`ENG-2503`, IT Operations, LEVEL-3) — Late-night
engineer on the Y2K project, works 13:00–23:00. Ivan is on-site
during the takeover; Hans's crew sweeps floor 25 at 22:10 and
Ivan is among the staff relocated to the floor-30 hostage group.

**Evan Rutherford** (`XG-9999`, formerly IT Operations) —
*Terminated April 1988.* Moved to a smaller firm in Torrance;
has no contact with Nakatomi. His badge, however, was never
revoked — a lapse that Robert Chen has been quietly using for
after-hours server-room access. Discovering this is the Ghost
Account side story (9099).

### 4.5 Admin & Finance

**Tamiko Yoshida** (`NP-2801`, Executive Support, LEVEL-3) —
Takagi's executive assistant. 44, at Nakatomi since 1979; runs
floor 28 and floor 30 scheduling with absolute precision.
Tamiko knows more about Takagi's calendar than Takagi does.
She is, by her own description, "off-limits to anyone below
clearance LEVEL-4, and barely tolerant of most of them."

**Ulysses Bekele** (`NP-2802`, Admin, LEVEL-2) — Accounting
clerk in Executive Support. 29, been at Nakatomi two years,
in serious financial trouble back home. Has been skimming
$40–$50 out of petty cash every few days under cover of
"office supplies" — side story 9050 (Petty Cash Skim). Was
one quarterly audit away from being discovered before the
takeover happened.

**Chen-Wei Liu** (`NP-2003`, Finance, LEVEL-3) — Head of Finance
floor 20. 51, Nakatomi since 1984, CPA and risk auditor. Chen-Wei
is the person who finalised the Dec 22 audit queue that flagged
Ulysses Bekele's expense pattern (side story 9050 payoff event).
The audit report was scheduled to be reviewed with HR on Dec 26.
It will not be.

### 4.6 Catering & Vendors

**Maria Santos** (`CAT-001`, Catering, LEVEL-1) — In-house
catering lead, 24. Runs the floor-30 pantry and organises the
Christmas party's food service along with the external vendor.
Maria spends the 20:00 party hour moving between the floor-30
kitchen and Conference Room A; her badge traffic is visible in
the dataset but routine.

**Eddie Pulaski** (`VND-001`, catering vendor, LEVEL-1) —
Contractor from **Saint-Germain Catering Services**, the
Beverly Hills catering firm Nakatomi uses for everything above
a 40-person party. Eddie has worked the Nakatomi Christmas
party three years running; he knows the loading-dock routine
and the floor-30 kitchen layout intimately.

**Halima Senghor** (`VND-004`, HVAC repair, LEVEL-2) — Contractor
from **Tri-Air Mechanical**, the firm Nakatomi uses for roof
chiller maintenance. Halima was last on-site on 22 December
for a zone 4 filter inspection (see § 5.4). The roof-level
HVAC cabinet she worked on carries the ROT-13 encoded message
that is the answer to Seal 4.

### 4.7 External Parties (referenced, not Nakatomi staff)

**John McClane** — NYPD, 38, detective third grade. Not an
employee. Arrives at the main lobby 21:42 PST on 24 December 1988
at Holly's invitation for the party. When Hans's crew takes the
building at 22:00 he is in the floor-30 bathroom drying off after
the limo ride. He stays. McClane's intercepted radio lines
(easter eggs 6199 and the morse D-A-D on channel security_primary)
are the dataset's only direct voice of the one Nakatomi outsider
nobody planned for.

**Al Powell** — LAPD, sergeant, 43. Nakatomi's post-1987 security
liaison. On Christmas Eve 1988 he is the first officer on the
scene after McClane's radio bursts. The 1987 radio channel that
Powell monitored during the Room 30-B incident is the same
frequency McClane ends up using in 1988 — not by chance; the
channel was never re-issued.

**Sam (Samantha) Thornburg** — LA news reporter, KFLW Channel 4.
Arrives at the building 23:40 for a live broadcast once the
scene becomes visible from outside. Thornburg does not appear in
the Nakatomi dataset; she is in the facilitator script only.

**Dwayne T. Robinson** — LAPD Deputy Chief. Arrives around
23:15 to "take command." Also does not appear in the Nakatomi
dataset; his part of the story plays out on external LAPD logs
that the players cannot query.

### 4.8 Antagonists — Hans's Crew

**Hans Gruber** (`HG-1988`, "External Contractor", EXTERNAL) — 42,
German, former East German State Security operative turned
international thief. Hans has a plausible cover (visiting
"Christian business partner" of Nakatomi's Frankfurt office) and
a badge that was issued legitimately two weeks earlier. The
whole rest of his crew travels under catering and service-vendor
cover identities.

**Karl Vreski** (`KA-2205`, External Contractor, EXTERNAL) —
Enforcer, 36. Hans's second and the only one of the crew who has
done significant physical violence by the time the heist begins.
Karl is the one who takes down the security team in the lobby.

**Theo** (`TH-0099`, External Contractor, EXTERNAL) — Technical
specialist, 31, American (the only American on Hans's crew). Theo
has been inside the building three times in the last month under
catering cover, and the proxy logs prove it (side story 9552).
Theo is the one who will crack the first six seals of the vault;
the seventh is the one Hans cannot solve without outside help —
that's why the FBI cutting the power is in the plan.

## 5. How This Lore Appears in Splunk

The generator seeds this lore through several on-screen surfaces a
player can discover via SPL. These events are **not** required to
finish the vault — they are pure world-building texture for the
curious analyst.

### 5.1 The intranet bulletin board

Seven intranet bulletins ship in 1988 posting dates, findable with:

```
index=nakatomi_building sourcetype=intranet:announcements
| table _time author department subject
| sort _time
```

Announcements cover the topics below. Each one reinforces a piece
of building lore in this document; none of them contain seal
codes or side-story answer codes.

- **Holiday Message from the CEO** — Takagi's annual Christmas note
  (Dec 20). References Q4 performance, the building's 13-year
  milestone, and the "sombre lesson of last December's incident."
- **Christmas Party RSVP — Final Call** — HR reminder with dress
  code and plus-one policy (Dec 22).
- **HVAC Zone 4 Service Window** — Facilities heads-up for the
  Tri-Air Mechanical visit on Dec 22 (§ 4.6).
- **Holiday Parking Adjustments** — B1/B2 changes for the party.
- **Scheduled Maintenance — Mail & Proxy** — IT's advance notice of
  Junko Hanada's Y2K pilot run (§ 4.4).
- **Building Anniversary Note** — the Plaza turns 13, with a nod
  to the 1985 elevator fire and the 1986 fire-suppression retrofit
  (§ 2.1).
- **Fire-Safety Drill Recap** — building-wide drill run on Dec 17.
  References "lessons still being applied from 1985 and 1987."

### 5.2 The HR archive — prior incidents

Two HR memos from 1985 and 1987 are referenced in the 1988 data
but not present as full events; their existence is hinted at in
the `intranet:hr` case_id format (`HR-85-…`, `HR-87-…`) used by
the side-story generator. Future releases may promote these to
first-class events.

### 5.3 The diary (Holly, Dec 24)

Easter egg `6024` — `sourcetype=intranet:diary`, folder `personal`.
Holly's first-person entry, discoverable by:

```
index=nakatomi_building sourcetype=intranet:diary author="Holly Gennero"
```

### 5.4 The schematic archive (the hidden door)

Easter egg `6404` — `sourcetype=intranet:facilities`. The 1987
Room 30-B reinforcement work is still documented in schematic
`FAC-30-v7`; the "reserved archive door" that emergency corridor
is described as.

### 5.5 Sensor & comms

HAL9000 proxy user-agent (`6911`), the morse "D-A-D" on the
legacy 1987 security channel (`6411`), and Hans's and McClane's
intercepted comms (`6199`, `6220`, `6252`, `6401`) all pull from
this document's character notes — nothing in the comms corpus
contradicts the bios above.

### 5.6 The absent story: why the 1988 party wasn't cancelled

Players will never find a direct answer to this. Inside Nakatomi,
the staff were aware that the Room 30-B incident had happened a
year earlier, and many quietly expected the 1988 Christmas party
to be downgraded to a private dinner. Holly Gennero is the one
who argued, hard, inside the executive committee, that cancelling
the party would be "letting that man win." Takagi agreed. The
decision was made on Nov 12, 1988, and recorded in the board
minutes — not surfaced in any event in this dataset, but
referenced in § 5.1's CEO holiday message.

## 6. Authoring Rules (for future contributors)

If you add a character, announcement, or side story:

1. Add the character to `generator/scenario.yaml` under
   `characters` or `npcs` so they are registered in the employee
   lookup.
2. Add a bio paragraph to § 4 of this document.
3. Do not contradict the two prior incidents (§ 2). The 1985
   elevator fire and the 1987 Room 30-B hostage incident are
   canon — anything you add must fit around them.
4. Announcement events must stay in
   `index=nakatomi_building sourcetype=intranet:announcements`
   and MUST NOT contain any 4-digit number that matches a seal
   code, trap code, side-story code, or easter-egg code. The
   generator fails loud if a collision is introduced.
5. Keep the tone dry and corporate; players should feel they are
   reading a real intranet, not a marketing pamphlet.
